Appeals and Writs in Northern California

A conviction, sentence, or court order is not always the final word. An appeal asks a higher court to review what happened in the trial court and correct legal errors. A writ asks a higher court to step in when an appeal is not available or would come too late. Tony Carlos handles appeals and writs arising from criminal cases, restraining order cases, immigration cases, and DMV license suspensions.

Appeals We Handle

Criminal Appeals

Misdemeanor and infraction appeals go to the Appellate Division of the Superior Court. Felony appeals from Sutter, Yuba, Sacramento, Placer, Butte, Colusa, Glenn, and Yolo Counties go to the California Court of Appeal, Third Appellate District, in Sacramento.

  • After a trial - Errors in the admission of evidence, jury instructions, sufficiency of the evidence, and prosecutorial misconduct
  • Sentencing errors - Unauthorized sentences, improper enhancements, and fines or fees imposed in error
  • Search and seizure - Denial of a motion to suppress evidence can be appealed even after a plea
  • Post-judgment orders - Denials of motions to vacate, resentencing petitions, and probation violation findings

Writs

Some rulings cannot wait for an appeal. A writ of mandate or prohibition can challenge certain pretrial rulings while the case is still pending, and a petition for writ of habeas corpus can raise problems that do not appear in the court record, such as new evidence or ineffective assistance of prior counsel.

Restraining Order Appeals

Domestic violence, civil harassment, workplace violence, and elder abuse restraining orders can be appealed. These orders can affect your housing, your job, your firearms, and your time with your children, so an order that was not supported by the evidence or was issued without a fair hearing is worth a close look. The deadline is generally 60 days after notice of entry of the order is served, and never more than 180 days after the order.

Immigration Appeals

If an immigration judge denied your case, you generally have 30 days to appeal to the Board of Immigration Appeals. If the Board rules against you, you generally have 30 days to file a petition for review with the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals. These deadlines are extremely difficult to extend.

DMV Suspension Writs

If the DMV upholds your license suspension after an administrative hearing, you can ask the Superior Court to review the decision through a petition for writ of mandate. The filing window is short, so contact us as soon as you receive the DMV's decision.

The Appeal Process

1. File the Notice of Appeal

We file the notice of appeal before the deadline runs, which protects your right to review.

2. Assemble the Record

The court prepares the clerk's transcript and the reporter's transcript of the hearings. The appeal is decided on this record.

3. Review the Record

We read the full record to identify every legal issue worth raising and give you an honest assessment.

4. Write the Opening Brief

We prepare the opening brief, which sets out the facts, the errors, and the legal authority for reversal.

5. Reply and Argument

After the other side responds, we file a reply brief and, when it helps your case, present oral argument.

6. Decision

The court issues a written decision. If it rules against you, we explain whether further review is available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an appeal a new trial?

No. There are no witnesses and no new evidence. The higher court reviews the record of what already happened and decides whether the trial court made a legal error that affected the outcome. Problems outside the record, such as new evidence, are usually raised through a petition for writ of habeas corpus instead.

Can I appeal if I pleaded guilty or no contest?

Sometimes. You can still appeal sentencing errors and the denial of a motion to suppress evidence. To challenge the validity of a felony plea itself, you generally need a certificate of probable cause from the trial court, which must be requested along with the notice of appeal.

Do I need to have hired you for my trial?

No. Many clients come to us after another attorney handled their case. A fresh set of eyes on the record is often an advantage on appeal.

How long does an appeal take?

It depends on the court and the size of the record. Misdemeanor appeals in the Appellate Division are often resolved within several months. Felony appeals in the Court of Appeal commonly take a year or more from the notice of appeal to a decision.

What happens if I miss the deadline?

In most cases a late notice of appeal means the right to appeal is lost. There are narrow exceptions, so call us right away even if you think the deadline has passed.
