Appeals and Writs in California

A conviction, sentence, or court order is not always the final word. An appeal asks a higher court to review what happened in the trial court and correct legal errors. A writ asks a higher court to step in when an appeal is not available or would come too late. Tony Carlos handles appeals and writs arising from criminal cases, restraining order cases, immigration cases, and DMV license suspensions.

Appeals We Handle

Criminal Appeals

We handle appeals in misdemeanor and felony cases, whether the case ended in a trial or a plea.

  • After a trial - Errors in how the trial was conducted and how the jury was instructed
  • Sentencing errors - Sentences or conditions that the law does not allow
  • Post-judgment orders - Rulings made after the case ended

Writs

Some rulings cannot wait for an appeal, and some problems never appear in the court record. A writ petition asks a higher court to step in on those issues.

Restraining Order Appeals

Restraining orders can affect your housing, your job, your firearms, and your time with your children. If you believe an order against you was wrong, we can review it and explain whether an appeal makes sense.

Immigration Appeals

If an immigration judge or the Board of Immigration Appeals ruled against you, we can review the decision and help you seek further review.

DMV Suspension Writs

If the DMV upholds your license suspension after a hearing, we can ask the Superior Court to review the decision. Contact us as soon as you receive the DMV's decision.

The Appeal Process

1. File the Notice of Appeal

We file the notice of appeal to protect your right to review.

2. Assemble the Record

The court prepares the transcripts and documents from your case.

3. Review the Record

We read the full record to identify every legal issue worth raising and give you an honest assessment.

4. Write the Opening Brief

We write the opening brief explaining the errors and why the result should change.

5. Reply and Argument

After the other side responds, we file a reply and present argument when it helps your case.

6. Decision

The court issues its decision, and we explain what it means and what options remain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an appeal a new trial?

Generally no. An appeal looks at what already happened in the trial court rather than hearing the case again. We can explain which kind of review fits your situation.

Do I need to have hired you for my trial?

No. Many clients come to us after another attorney handled their case. A fresh set of eyes on the record is often an advantage on appeal.

What happens if I miss the deadline?

Missing a deadline can end your right to appeal. Call us right away, even if you think the deadline may have passed.
